You could waste tens of thousands of dollars renting a wrong space for your salon.
Hi, I'm Theresa, a salon owner in Toronto.
Check these 6 things before putting down the deposit.
First, zoning. Don't assume that any retail space allows a nail salon.
Confirm it's permitted for personal care services before you sign the lease.
Second, plumbing and hot water. One of the most expensive items.
Check the water and drain line, how many pedicure chairs the space can support,
and the hot water tank capacity.
Third, electrical and ventilation. Salons run a lot of equipment.
If the system can't handle the load, the upgrade bill will get huge.
Four, the real cost of rent.
Add TMI, taxes, utilities, insurance, and the annual increase to the base rent.
Fifth, rent free time.
Permits and construction take months, so try to negotiate free rent during renovation.
Six, lease term. Too short and you can't recoup your
costs. If it's too long, it's risky if the location underperforms.
So check the renewal, relocation, demolition, and personal warranty clause.
A good location isn't always the cheapest one.
It's the one with the right customer, reasonable cost, and room for you to grow.
