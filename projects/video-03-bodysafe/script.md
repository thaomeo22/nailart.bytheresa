The health inspector can walk in on any day. Here's what they check first.

Hi, I'm Theresa, a salon owner in Toronto. Here, nail salons are inspected by BodySafe, under Ontario Regulation 136/18. Four things to get right:

One: single-use items. Paper files, buffers, toe separators — one client, then the garbage. Even if the package says "sanitizable."

Two: reusable tools. Clean first, then disinfect, between every client. And you need a separate sink for cleaning tools — not your hand-wash sink.

Three: your disinfectant. It needs a DIN on the label, and you use it exactly the way the label says. A UV box doesn't count — it's only for storing tools that are already disinfected.

Four: records. Every client — name, phone number, date, service, and who did it. Keep them for three years, with at least one year at the salon.

Treat every day like inspection day.

Follow me for more salon business tips.

## Caption đăng
In Toronto, nail salons are inspected by BodySafe under Ontario Regulation 136/18. Single-use items, cleaning + disinfecting between clients, a DIN disinfectant (not a UV box), and your client records — get these four right and inspection day is just another day.

## Nguồn
- Ontario Regulation 136/18, Personal Service Settings — s.4 (posting inspection results), s.8(3) (separate reprocessing sink), s.10(4)–(5) (clean & disinfect reusable equipment between each use; discard single-use items), s.11(2) (disinfectant must have a Health Canada DIN/NPN, used per manufacturer's instructions), s.14 (disinfection records; kept on site 1 year + 2 more years): https://www.ontario.ca/laws/regulation/180136
